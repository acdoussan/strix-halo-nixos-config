3 · S1 — latency vs prompt length; checkpoint-cache gain

Framing: TTFT here is almost entirely a cache phenomenon: TTFT ≈ queue + (uncached prompt tokens ÷ ≈1400 tok/s). On a hit, only the new chunk (or nothing) is prefilled → sub-second to ~1 s. On a miss, you pay context size linearly — that is the whole of it. Scheduling contributes queue time only (§4/§4b).

Cold prefill ≈ 0.88–1.47 k tok/s; TTFT @200k ≈ 150 s. Repeat passes with different content show no KV reuse at ≥64k — expected from the tooling limitation, compounded by gufo refusing long-prompt RAM captures (capture_refused reason=byte_capacity ×663; a 64k snapshot ≈2.33 GiB vs the ledger pinned at 10 GiB). 4 · S2 — concurrency curve

System throughput plateaus at ≈30–35 tok/s from concurrency 1 (decode-bound; MTP acceptance median 61%, range 42–96%). Beyond 2 sessions p99 TTFT grows ≈linearly (1.9 s → 110 s at c16) for ≈0 throughput gain: max useful concurrency = 2 (= sessions). 0 errors, all 15 runs. Reading the flat system line: it is a capacity plateau, not a per-agent promise — additional agents do not add decode capacity, they queue. The per-agent cost of adding agents is quantified in §4b.

Decoding is a fixed pie shared by at most 2 resident sessions: solo an agent gets ~41 tok/s; with a batch partner both get ~28 (bandwidth-limited width-2 batch: 2×28 ≈ 56 while decoding, ~34 avg including prefill/idle gaps — only ~11% more than solo). Adding agents beyond two leaves the at-head-of-line rate unchanged (~27) but each agent's effective rate falls ≈proportionally as queue time grows (S2 back-to-back: 2.8 tok/s at c16; conversations with 5 s think-time: ~11.7 → ~7.2 → ~3.5 tok/s/agent at nc 2→4→8). 10 · Top-3 tuning recommendations

  1. Fix long-prompt checkpoint capture (the real blocker). Evidence: capture_refused reason=byte_capacity ×663 + persistence_refused reason=min_step — one-shot ≥64 k prompts reach zero checkpoints on either tier (nearest_checkpoint_tokens=0/65581 at 200k replays) — while the one full restore returned 204,847 tokens in ≈0.1–5 s. A 64 k snapshot costs 2.33 GiB, so capacity for 2–3 concurrent restores exists; the gates, not the bytes, are refusing. On build 3373114 grid density/min-step are not CLI knobs — raise capture policy upstream with the vendor; the CLI-side levers are the budgets in (2). Gain: −150 s cold re-prefill per repeat/turn ≥64 k.
  2. Re-budget the cache; a 3rd session is the wrong lever; doubling disk is a cheap second-order yes. The 10 GiB RAM ledger sat pinned all series; the disk tier filled 256 GiB (≈250 GiB churned in 5.5 h of test load) and now evicts at the cap. A 3rd 262 k session costs +6.2 GiB KV — exactly the starved resource — against ≈11% batching gain from session 2 and a projected ≈5% from session 3 (plan §6: 3×262 k borderline, 4× impossible); sessions are concurrency insurance that queueing already provides. 3–4 sessions only pay if per-session context drops to ≤131 k, which breaks the 256 k requirement. Doubling disk (256→512 GiB) costs no RAM, root FS has 981 GiB free, and lengthens retention distance for fleet continuations — but does not fix (1), which gates captures upstream of disk. CLI validation note: --cache-ram-bytes explicit values are capped at free-RAM−4 GiB at startup, so a raise must be planned around boot-time headroom. Price the split with the S6(a)-OFF arm + replay.
  3. Keep MTP spec-decode ON; admit at most 2 concurrent streams per client pool. Confirmed by direct A/B (§7): MTP wins every regime — solo decode ≈2× without it (20.9 vs 40.6 tok/s) and width-2 still favors ON (28.2 vs 21.5 per agent; acceptance 61% median). Beyond 2 sessions throughput is flat (≈34 tok/s) while p99 TTFT grows 6–50× (S2 c4–c16; S3 nc8 queue 100 s). Prefer client-side admission over server oversubscription.
